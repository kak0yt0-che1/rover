Model notes

What the Mission Design simulation computes, what it simplifies, and which numbers come from NASA pages. Everything in src/md_model.js is described here. The game is a teaching model: its numbers are not data for any real spacecraft or rocket.

2. What the simulation computes

Mass. The total mass must not exceed the launcher's capacity to the chosen orbit (capacity is zero for orbits a launcher cannot reach).

mcraft=∑imi≤mcap(launcher, orbit)

Power. Average power from the panels depends on panel area A, a wing multiplier k, a fixed peak flux and the share of the orbit in sunlight fsun. It must cover the average load.

Pgen=A·k·190·fsun≥Pload=3+∑ipidi+ptxfcontact+0.05pprop

Units: watts and square metres. The constant 190 W per square metre and the 3 W core load are game values. di is the duty cycle of instrument i.

Battery through eclipse. During eclipse the battery supplies the load; the design is rejected if more than 80 percent of the battery would be used each orbit.

Pload·teclipse/60Cbattery≤0.8

Eclipse time teclipse is in minutes, battery capacity Cbattery in watt-hours.

Data and communications. Data produced per day, and what the radio can send during ground-station contact:

G=∑igidiD=R·tcontact·608·1000ρ=min(1,DG)

R in megabits per second, tcontact in minutes per day, G and D in gigabytes per day. Data beyond the bus storage is lost.

Budget and time. Hardware, launch and ground cost are added and compared with the scenario budget (scaled by difficulty). Weeks to launch are the sum of build weeks and the launcher's wait, compared with the launch window.

Science. Instruments of the kind the scenario wants count in full, other kinds one third. The orbit multiplies everything by how useful it is for the objective.

S=forbit∑isidiwi

wi is 1 for a matching instrument and 0.3 otherwise.

Delta-v (background). The rocket equation links the speed change a thruster can give to its propellant. The game does not compute it: each thruster has a fixed delta-v and the Moon scenario needs a minimum.

Δv=Isp·g0·ln(m0mf)

3. Random events

A seeded random generator rolls each day. The same seed gives the same mission, so a replay is exact. Events: solar flare (panel output drops for 3 days), ground-station outage (no downlink for 2 days), instrument glitch (no measurements for 2 days), one wing opening only halfway (at launch), and launch failure (depends on the rocket's reliability). Chances are scaled by difficulty.

5. Simplifications, stated plainly

  • The orbit is a set of numbers (sunlight share, eclipse minutes, contact minutes). No orbit is propagated.
  • Ground contact is minutes per day; there is no signal delay or weather.
  • The thermal, attitude-control and onboard-computer subsystems are not modelled.
  • Launchers are generic classes with made-up capacity, cost and reliability.
  • A mission lasts 30 days in daily steps. The battery is tracked once a day.
  • Several designs can succeed; there is no single perfect answer, but the model is far from a real mission analysis.
